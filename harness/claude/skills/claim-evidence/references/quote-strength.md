# An attributed claim must not exceed its quote

Write the weakest sentence the quote supports. A cited claim carries the source's authority, so
strengthening it while attributing it launders your own inference into someone else's accepted
fact — and every later reader treats it as settled.

## Procedure

1. **Paste the source sentence next to your draft** before writing the claim into the artifact.
   Not the line number — the words.
2. **Underline the quantifier in each.** These are the drift sites:
   - a bound on *kind* → a bound on *count*: "depends on another block only for what it cannot
     recompute" does **not** mean "only one block is upstream"
   - "should" / "prefers" → "must"
   - a bound on one case → "always" / "never"
   - an enumeration of examples → a closed set
3. **Enumerate the instances the strong form forbids, and look for one.** If the draft says
   "only X", list what else could qualify and check each: other sources in the same corpus, and
   decisions you added yourself after the source was written. A source that predates your own
   decisions cannot rule them out.
4. **Rewrite to the quote's own axis.** Keep the source's dimension — kind, direction, scope —
   instead of converting it to a count or an absolute.
5. **Attribute only the quoted part.** Your inference can stay in the artifact; it goes in its
   own sentence, unattributed, or as a decision note of your own.

## Trap

Adding a summary line that reads more useful than the source — a crisp "X is the only Y" beats a
hedged clause, so the strong form feels like a service to the reader. It is the one sentence they
will quote back, and it is yours, not the source's. The check costs one paste.
